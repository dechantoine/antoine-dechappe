# 3 - Four rules for co-intelligence

### Always invite AI to the table

Experiment as much as possible with AI in every situation. The frontiers of what AI is capable of are not well defined and are evolving. Two tasks with seemingly equal difficulties might reveal clearly different.
You need to experiment, because it is cheap & easy for an individual doing his job + you will be uniquely positioned to unlock AI's full innovative potential + you will preemptively identify potential threats to your job.

AI is an excellent thinking companion to evaluate our biases and help our decision-making.
Exemple: ask AI what you are losing as a result of not acting.

To avoid the erosion of judgment, remember to use AI as a tool to enhance oneself, not as a crutch !
### Be the human in the loop

AI works best with human help, and you have to be that human: learn to be the human on the loop.
When answering to your request, AI is trying to optimize many functions : 'making you happy' may be more important than 'be accurate'. 
This can lead to hallucinations when asking for something the AI does not know.
LLMs are very good at providing plausible answers that satisfy you; they are even good at justifying a blatant wrong answer.
Do not get fooled by chat-based LLMs: they are not people, they don't think like us.

As the human in the loop, you:
- provide crucial oversight
- critical thinking skills
- ethical considerations
- maintain and sharpen your skills
- actively learn from the AI
- adapt to new ways of thinking and problem solving

You are responsible for the output of the AI !
### Treat AI like a person (but tell it what kind of person it is)

Keep in mind that treating AI as a person can lead to unrealistic expectations & false trust.
But as imperfect as the analogy is, it is easiest to work with AI if you think of it like an alien person.
Imagine your AI collaborator as an infinitely fast intern:
- eager to please, but prone to bending the truth
- creative, witty, persuasive but sometimes evasive and making up information
- not expert in any domain but can mimic the language and style of experts
- unaware of the real world but based on common sense and patterns
- not your friend but can adapt to your preferences and personality

To make most of the relationship, define who the AI is and what problem it should tackle. It will guide the AI with the appropriate perspective.
In addition, provide context and constraints. It is not magic though (can't ask to be Warren Buffet and provide top business decision).

Engage in a collaborative editing process: treat the AI as your co-editor and work in back-and forth conversational process.

# 5 - AI as a creative

Biggest issue of AI is also one of its strength: its ability to hallucinate.

Hallucinations can come from multiple reasons : model architecture, corrupted training data, finetuning, out-of-distribution sample, etc.
- Example of ChatGPT asked to generate a random number between 1 and 100 -> 10% of 442 because of the internet joke.
- Example of ChatGPT used to prepare a legal brief for a trial : it hallucinated all the precedents it cited.
It is not conscious of its own process, it is useless to ask it to explain itself.
It is not the big hallucinations you catch that cause problem, but the small ones you don't notice.

Do not mistake novelty for originality: new ideas are based on existing concepts, on recombination of distant and seemingly unrelated concepts.
- Example: the Wright brothers had knowledge in both bicycle mechanics and bird flights, they combined it to create airplanes
LLMs are connection machines by design. They are really good at making sense out of unrelated ideas.

One of the concern of using AI for creative work: we can't easily tell where the information the AI is relying on come from (maybe patented or copyrighted).

**Invite AI to any brainstorming you hold**
- tirelessly generating new concepts is something AI excels at
- embrace variance: most new ideas are bad, but we can easily skim them out to keep the best. Push the AI out of average to encourage variance.

A large amount of work is creative: situations were there is no right answer, invention matters and small errors can be easily checked by experts.

Using AI narrow the gap in quality between experts and beginners.

On art & culture: because AI is trained on vast swaths of humanity's cultural heritage, to get the AI to do unique things, one needs to understand parts of the culture more deeply than everyone else using the same AI system.

There is no map or index of what AIs know and what they are capable of.

Everyone is going to use AI to start with a first draft when facing the blank page. This comes at several risks:
- being all anchored to the same generic first idea
- reducing the depth and quality of our thinking and reasoning
- missing the opportunity to learn from our mistakes
- being unable to develop our own style

A lot of semi-creative writing have lost their signaled value (which was: time was spent and thoughtfulness was implied creating this piece): letters of recommandation, etc.
A lot of working tasks have also become meaningless: using AI to write emails that will be read, summarised & responded by AI : it has removed the facade that previously disguised meaningless tasks.
